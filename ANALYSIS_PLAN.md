## 1. Units and period

- **Primary city frame.** Urban centres in the GHSL Urban Centre Database R2024A, version 1.2 (file `GHS_UCDB_GLOBE_R2024A_V1_2.zip`, data last update 15 May 2026, CC BY 4.0; EXTRACTED, `01_data/raw/ghsl_ucdb/readme_V1_2.txt`) with a GHSL population of at least 1,000,000 in 2020 (field `GH_POP_TOT_2020`): 499 urban centres, minus those without temperature data (rule 6e). Polygons from layer `GHSL_UCDB_THEME_GHSL_GLOBE_R2024A`, reprojected from Mollweide to WGS84.
- **Sensitivity frames.** (i) The 183 national capitals flagged in the database (`GC_UCM_CAP = 1`), of any size; (ii) primary-frame urban centres with at least 5,000,000 inhabitants in 2020. (V0_1 named C40 member cities; no C40 list is in hand, so capitals replace them.) Five non-sovereign capitals (Cayenne, St Helier, the northern part of Nicosia, Mamoudzou, Saint-Denis) have no WHO region or income group and are labelled "Unassigned".
- **Grouping.** WHO region from the WHO Global Health Observatory country dimension (API, fetched 14 Nov 2026); World Bank income group from `CLASS.xlsx` (fetched 14 Nov 2026); Venezuela is "Unclassified".
- **Population weights.** GHSL urban centre population in 2020 for every year (fixed weights, so trends reflect hazard change, not growth). Sensitivity: year-matched population interpolated linearly between the GHSL epochs 2000, 2005, 2010, 2015, 2020 and 2025 (2021 to 2023 use the GHSL 2025 projection).
- **Period.** 1 January 2000 to 31 December 2023 (range of the fire PM2.5 product).

## 2. Exposures

- **Fire-sourced PM2.5.** Hu et al. 2025 daily product, 2000 to 2023 (Zenodo doi:10.5281/zenodo.15493914, GFED4.1s-based; doi:10.5281/zenodo.15496596, QFED2.5-based; CC BY 4.0). The fields were simulated by GEOS-Chem at 2° by 2.5° and bilinearly downscaled to 0.25° (EXTRACTED, `inherited_P44/evidence/fulltext/Hu_2025_fire_pm25_dataset.pdf`, journal p. 3743), so neighbouring cities share largely interpolated values and city series are not independent. **Primary: the mean of the GFED-based and QFED-based estimates**, following the authors' recommendation "using the average of the fire-sourced [PM2.5] from both inventories to indicate the mean state while using their difference as the range of uncertainties" (EXTRACTED, same file, p. 3752). The GFED-based and QFED-based estimates are reported as the product range; neither is a uniform lower or upper bound (GFED exceeded QFED in the 2023 Canadian fires, same page). City value: area-weighted mean of the 0.25° cells whose boxes intersect the urban centre polygon (weight = intersection area times cos latitude); masked cells are set missing; a city-day needs at least half of the polygon weight. Daily values are stamped 12:00 and treated as UTC days (INFERRED from the time strings).
- **The product holds the fire-sourced component only.** Every smoke threshold and band below applies to fire-sourced PM2.5, whereas the WHO guideline level and the US AQI breakpoints refer to total PM2.5. The counts are therefore days on which fire smoke alone reaches a total-PM2.5 reference: a lower bound of activation on total readings in cities with background pollution. The all-source definition of Wu et al. 2026 (all-source PM2.5 at or above 15 µg/m3 with a fire share of at least 50%) cannot be reproduced because the archives carry only the fire field (checked: variables `time`, `lat`, `lon`, `PM25`); this is a stated limitation. The GFED-based product was 55% lower than an independent US smoke product (EXTRACTED, reading note of Hu 2025, Validation): a level bias that lowers counts; stated as a limitation with its direction.
- **Temperature.** NOAA CPC Global Unified daily maximum and minimum 2 m temperature, 0.5°, gauge-based, land only (files `tmax.YYYY.nc`, `tmin.YYYY.nc`, https://downloads.psl.noaa.gov/Datasets/cpc_global_temp/, 1991 to 2023; NOAA data). City value: area-weighted mean of the 0.5° cells intersecting the polygon, renormalised over cells with data, with the adjacent-ring fallback of rule 6a. ERA5 is not used as the primary source because it needs a Copernicus account, which this run cannot create; it enters as a validation for 20 cities (Section 6).
- **Baseline for percentiles.** 1991 to 2020, all days of the year, city-specific (ASSUMPTION: the most recent complete 30-year period before the study end; no WMO normals document is in hand, so no external authority is claimed). Precedents: fixed historical baselines (Rosenthal et al. 2022, 1970 to 2010, p. 4) and study-period baselines (Wu et al. 2026, 2000 to 2019). Sensitivity: 2000 to 2023 baseline.

## 3. Definitions (primary; sensitivity in brackets). Smoke values are fire-sourced PM2.5, 24-hour means.

- **Heat day:** Tmax at or above the city-specific 95th percentile of daily Tmax over the baseline (90th; 97.5th; absolute 35 °C, an ASSUMPTION with indirect support only).
- **Smoke day:** fire-sourced PM2.5 at or above 15 µg/m3, the WHO 2021 24-hour guideline level (EXTRACTED, WHO AQG 2021, p. 88) that Annex 1 names as the operational reference (WHO Europe 2026, p. 260) (5, an ASSUMPTION since 5 µg/m3 is the annual guideline; 25, interim target 4, p. 88; 35.5, lower bound of the US AQI "unhealthy for sensitive groups" category, EPA-454/B-24-002 Table 6, p. 14).
- **Compound day:** heat day and smoke day on the same date. Sensitivity: smoke on the heat day or the next day (applies to smoke days, compound days and conflict nights, not to matrix cells).
- **Matrix cell.** Heat tier H0 (Tmax below the 90th percentile), H1 (90th to below 95th), H2 (95th to below 97.5th), H3 (97.5th or above), by fire-sourced smoke band S0 (below the smoke threshold, primary 15 µg/m3), S1 (threshold to below 35.5), S2 (35.5 to below 55.5), S3 (55.5 or above). The 35.5 and 55.5 bounds are the lower bounds of the US AQI "unhealthy for sensitive groups" and "unhealthy" categories, unchanged in the 2024 revision (EXTRACTED, EPA-454/B-24-002, Table 6, p. 14). The smoke-threshold sensitivities move the S1 lower bound with the smoke-day threshold.
- **Action levels (fixed here, before counting; contribution B, element E1).** For cells with H at least 1 and S at least 1, joint score = H + S: score 2 is **Joint level 1 (joint advisory)**; 3 or 4 is **Joint level 2 (joint warning)**; 5 or 6 is **Joint level 3 (joint emergency)**. Cells with one hazard only are single-hazard days handled by the existing heat or smoke plan. **Matrix activation day** = any day at joint level 1 or higher. The additive score is an ASSUMPTION encoding that each hazard escalates the response to the other; it draws on a Californian study whose authors suggest a joint warning system "using more stringent standards (e.g., 15 µg/m3 instead of 35 µg/m3)" on hot days (Chen et al. 2024, Science Advances, re-verified on the page at stage 5), which supports a lower smoke trigger on hot days but not the equal step sizes. Sensitivities: tiers on an absolute scale (32, 35, 38 °C); joint level = max(H, S); activation requiring heat tier H2 or H3; smoke threshold 5, 25, 35.5 µg/m3 for the S1 lower bound.
- **Cooling need.** Tmax on the heat day at or above 26 °C (ASSUMPTION: the upper end of indoor heat thresholds in hand, 26 °C in Kenny et al. 2019 and 25 to 28 °C in temperate Europe in Lomas and Li 2023, p. 485; below it a home is unlikely to overheat). Sensitivity 24 and 28 °C.
- **Night after the heat day.** The night that follows heat day d is represented by Tmin of day d+1 (the morning minimum ending that night); fire-sourced PM2.5 counts for that night if the smoke threshold is met on d or d+1, because the night spans two UTC days. Sensitivity: same-date Tmin and smoke on d only.
- **Ventilation night:** Tmin of d+1 below the night limit L, so outdoor air falls below the indoor limit for part of the night and open windows can cool the home. Primary L = 24 °C, the night-time room temperature in WHO Europe heat advice ("below 32 °C during the day and 24 °C during the night", WHO Europe 2011, p. 5; EXTRACTED; presented as WHO advice, not a health threshold, since WHO 2018 states the maximum indoor temperature needs research). Sensitivity L = 22 °C (a stricter margin) and 26 °C (Kenny 2019; Lomas and Li 2023). Using Tmin as a proxy for "outdoors cooler than indoors" is an ASSUMPTION (Health Canada advice on opening windows when outdoors is cooler is EXTRACTED, web).
- **Conflict night:** heat day d with cooling need, smoke on d or d+1, and a ventilation night: opening windows imports smoke, keeping them closed forgoes the only cooling available without air conditioning. **No-relief night:** the same hot smoky day whose night stays at or above L (neither option cools). Reported also as the share of hot smoky days with cooling need whose night allows ventilation.

## 4. Outputs

- Per city and year: counts of heat, smoke, compound days; days per matrix cell and joint level; conflict nights; no-relief nights; valid days.
- Summaries for the primary frame, 2019 to 2023: population-weighted mean annual days per resident with a 95% cluster bootstrap interval over cities (1,000 resamples, seed 44); median and interquartile range across cities; maximum; person-days per year; **number of cities with a mean of at least one per year** (primary city-count measure) and number with any (secondary); by WHO region and World Bank income group; top cities.
- Expected compound days if heat and smoke days were independent within each city-year (heat days times smoke days divided by valid days), as a descriptive comparator for seasonal co-occurrence.
- Trends: (a) **headline contrast** = ratio of population-weighted means, 2019 to 2023 against 2000 to 2004, with a cluster bootstrap interval; 2003 and 2023 values are descriptive only; (b) global population-weighted annual series: Theil-Sen slope with 95% interval and the Hamed-Rao modified Mann-Kendall test (original test as sensitivity); (c) per city: Poisson regression of annual counts on year for cities with at least 10 events in 2000 to 2023, Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate 0.05, reporting increases, decreases and the number tested (results are spatially correlated, so counts of cities are descriptive). Per-city Theil-Sen slopes are not computed.
- Figures: (1) world map of mean annual conflict nights per city, 2019 to 2023; (2) matrix heat map of population-weighted days per resident in each of the 16 cells; (3) population-weighted annual series of activation days and conflict nights by WHO region; (4) the specification figure (matrix with joint levels and conflict rule). Tables: specification (contribution B); results by region and income group with sensitivity ranges. Supplement: city-level table, sensitivity table, validation, data provenance, this plan with deviations.

## 5. Uncertainty and sensitivity

One-at-a-time sensitivity around the primary definition, each reported next to the primary result: product (GFED-based, QFED-based); heat day (90th, 97.5th, 35 °C); smoke threshold and S1 bound (5, 25, 35.5); cooling need (24, 28 °C); night limit (22, 26 °C); same-date night; matrix tiers absolute; joint level = max(H, S); activation requiring H2 or above; baseline 2000 to 2023; smoke lag 0 to 1 day; frames (capitals; 5 million or more); year-matched population; trend contrasts excluding CPC-inhomogeneous cities (rule 6f). Each sensitivity reports compound days, activation days, level 3 days, conflict nights and no-relief nights with their ratios. The bootstrap interval reflects sampling of cities, not exposure-model error; the product range is reported beside it.

## 6. Data quality rules and validation

Rules (a) to (e) were set from inspection of the temperature series only; (f) and (g) were added after the methods review, before any fire data were extracted.
- (a) CPC: a city-day uses every intersecting cell with data and, when none has data, the equal-weight mean of the adjacent ring of cells (the CPC land mask changed in 2006 and dropped the cells of several coastal cities, for example Hong Kong and Manama). Fire PM2.5: a city-day needs at least half of the polygon weight; masked values are missing, and each yearly file is checked for 365 or 366 consecutive days and a plausible range.
- (b) A temperature value is set missing when it deviates more than 15 °C and more than 8 robust standard deviations (1.4826 times the median absolute deviation) from the city's calendar-month median, and both values when Tmin exceeds Tmax; counts reported.
- (c) A day counts only when fire PM2.5, Tmax and Tmin are all present (and Tmin of d+1 for night outcomes).
- (d) Cities with fewer than 95% valid city-days in 2000 to 2023 stay in the 2019 to 2023 summaries but leave trend contrasts and trend tests; listed in the supplement. The same completeness rule applies to regional and income-group contrasts.
- (e) Cities with fewer than 95% valid Tmax and Tmin days in 2019 to 2023 leave every frame (named in the supplement).
- (f) CPC homogeneity: a city is flagged when its own-cell weight with data changes by more than 0.2 between 2003 and 2010 or more than 1% of its days use the ring fallback (per city-year log `01_data/processed/cpc_coverage_log.csv`); flagged cities are excluded in a trend sensitivity.
- (g) The share of valid city-days is reported per city in the supplement.

Validation:
- Fire PM2.5: correlation of GFED-based and QFED-based city series and the share of city-days with GFED above QFED; known events checked (Sydney November 2019 to January 2020, Moscow July to August 2010, Vancouver August 2018, Seattle September 2020, Singapore and Kuala Lumpur September to October 2015, New York early June 2023, Californian cities August to October 2020 and Sacramento November 2018).
- Comparison with Wu et al. 2026: the share of heatwave person-days (Tmax above the 90th percentile on at least 2 consecutive days) with fire-sourced PM2.5 at or above 15 µg/m3, 2000 to 2019, set beside their reported 3.9% for "sustained fire-sourced air pollution" within heatwave days (EXTRACTED, Wu 2026, Results); definitions differ (their all-source rule, daily mean temperature, global grid), so the comparison is of order of magnitude only.
- Californian cities in 2020: heat, smoke and compound days per city, set beside the statewide co-exposure reported by Rosenthal et al. 2022 for 2020 (source in hand). Cleland et al. 2025 (British Columbia) is not in hand and is not used.
- Temperature: CPC against Open-Meteo ERA5 for 20 cities, 2019 to 2023 (random sample, seed 44, three per WHO region then random): correlation and bias of Tmax and Tmin, agreement of heat-day flags (each source's own 2019 to 2023 95th percentile, Cohen's kappa) and of ventilation-night flags at 22, 24 and 26 °C. A long ERA5 pull to test trend homogeneity was not feasible within the open API's request limits (INFERRED from its published limits); rule 6f is the guard.
